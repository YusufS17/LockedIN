## What the user is asking for

- A real product they can play with, test and use with friends; the hackathon is over.
- A Habbo-inspired, retro, cosy/lofi social study world with expressive, customisable avatars.
- Private group study rooms where people see what others are studying and aggregate focus states, with joint accountability.
- Solo rooms as well as group rooms, improving visibly through real study effort.
- Actual avatar customisation and room decorating, with a polished studio-quality visual ambition. Generic placeholder shapes and superficial customisation do not meet the brief.
- A visual, iterative process using images and inspiration, followed by an excellent MVP and incremental features.
- Preserve useful existing work, but do not treat Claude's existing artwork or UI as an accepted quality target.
- Communicate in plain language for a non-technical user. Make results visible and keep progress/roadmaps saved.

## Interpretation of the supplied GPT brief

Retain: private rooms, subject/goal setup, fast presets, supportive default, optional competitive comparison, aggregate-only accountability, quiet focus sessions, meaningful results and persistent earned progression. The productivity shell should be modern and readable, with pixel art concentrated in the game world.

The old fixed 25-minute £5 demo contract is historical vertical-slice guidance, not the required front door for the current product. Keep any test-stake flow optional and clearly simulated. No real-money movement.

An offline demo with seeded companions can establish the local loop, but it does not fulfil the user's eventual requirement to study with real friends. Private multiplayer remains a required product milestone, not an optional aesthetic extra.

Do not claim native blocking or tracking before implementation and device verification. Do not expose specific apps, sites, contacts, messages or notification contents to friends. Scoring must be transparent and based on measured events. Public teasing/comparison requires opt-in.

## Delivery sequence

1. Keep the now-visible app and verified local journeys working as new features arrive.
2. Compare it with the user's visual references. If the original references cannot be located, ask the user to attach them again before asserting fidelity to them.
3. Iterate on one personal room, one four-seat room and one customisable avatar family, with visible before/after evidence.
4. Preserve the verified offline study-to-earned-room-item loop, including truthful outcome data.
5. Deliver private friend sessions with genuine shared room state, then verified native focus controls.

Timer/reward reliability supports this work; it does not establish the visual or multiplayer goals. A successful build is not proof of a polished product.
